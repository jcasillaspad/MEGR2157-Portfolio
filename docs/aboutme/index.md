**Juan Luis Casillas Padilla**

My interest in robotics is what led me to mechanical engineering. I became interested in how mechanical systems are designed and how individual components work together to create a functioning machine. Engineering also fits my strengths in problem solving because I enjoy working through a problem, testing possible solutions, and finding ways to improve a design. As I continue developing as a mechanical engineer, I want to become someone who can take a design from an idea to a working mechanical system. I am particularly interested in designing and building mechanical components that must function reliably in real-world applications. I want to develop stronger skills in CAD, mechanics, manufacturing, and engineering analysis so that my designs are supported by more than just trial and error. My goal is to become an engineer who can understand why a design works, identify why it fails, and make informed changes based on evidence. Throughout this semester, I also want to improve how I document my engineering process so that another engineer can understand and reproduce my work. This portfolio will show that development through the projects and decisions I document.

**What does it mean to defend an engineering decision, and do you currently know how to do it?**

To defend an engineering decision means being able to explain why a particular solution was selected using evidence, calculations, requirements, and engineering principles rather than simply saying it was the best option. At this point, I understand the basic idea of defending a decision, but I do not think I can consistently do it at the level of a practicing engineer. I still need to develop my ability to compare alternatives, identify the most important criteria, and use engineering analysis to support my choices. I expect this portfolio to help me improve that skill throughout the semester.
